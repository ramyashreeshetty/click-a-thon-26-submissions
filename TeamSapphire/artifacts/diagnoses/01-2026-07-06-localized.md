### In plain language

This was localized, not broad. Video ad format moved -18.0% against -9.4% globally, carrying 100% of the unexplained movement and 29% of the whole incident. Two overlapping cuts show the same weight: Android 12 moved -17.2% against -9.3% globally (73% of unexplained movement, 20% of the incident) and iPhone 14 moved -22.3% against -9.2% globally (66% of unexplained movement, 18% of the incident). The shape on video eCPM was gradual, not a single step: onset at 2026-07-06 00:00:00 took it from 6.520218 to 6.102399 (-0.417818), with the largest single hour carrying only 34.31% of the change. It recovered gradually from 2026-07-10 23:00:00 (4.126668 to 4.297747, +0.171079, largest hour 15.48%). The onset lands exactly on a day boundary. Interpretation: a gradual move onto a day boundary is consistent with a rollout, campaign flight, config push or nightly job that schedules in days, rather than an abrupt single change or an organic failure.

Checked and cleared: publisher_tier was uniform across all 3 values (each within 0.5% of the global -9.1%, largest outlier only 1.1% of the incident); category uniform across 7 values (within 3.7% of -9.3%, largest outlier 0.8%); campaign_type uniform across 4 values (within 9.1% of -9.1%, largest outlier 0.6%); vertical uniform across 8 values (within 9.1% of -9.1%, largest outlier 0.3%). Region and country were inconclusive — NAM and US lead their dimensions but each contributes 0% of the unexplained movement, below the 50% concentration bar, so the movement is spread rather than concentrated. Fill rate and render rate held steady throughout, so supply and delivery mechanics were intact. Next step: look at video demand and pricing on the 2026-07-06 day boundary — campaign flight starts/ends, bidder or floor config pushed that day, and any progressive rollout touching Android 12 and iPhone 14 video inventory — and check what changed around 2026-07-10 23:00:00 that let it recover.
