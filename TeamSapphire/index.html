Where it fell short, stated plainly. Our attribution asks whether a segment’s metric moved beyond what its size explains. In a mix shift no segment’s metric moves — only the weights do, so the system reached for correlates of the incoming traffic instead of the shift itself. Catching that needs a share-of-volume decomposition alongside the metric one, and we did not build it. The consolidation also merged the two incidents into one on the full-window run.

How it works

Raw MergeTree → three dictionaries resolving nine dimensions at insert time → two SummingMergeTree rollups. The obvious fully-crossed rollup was built first and measured at 7,247,816 rows from 7.2M events — it compressed nothing. The unpivoted grain that replaced it is 53,760 rows, 167× smaller. One investigation runs 131 queries over 1,217,174,868 rows in ~53s of ClickHouse time.
